## What was done

This project was commissioned by Exalt Editing. InfoDesignLab partnered with Roz Pidcock and her team, whose role was to produce the reports. All content is authored by a team of scientists from each respective country.

The IPCC sixth assessment cycle (AR6) is the starting point for the authors. The goal is to translate the AR6 findings to make them relevant and accessible to a range of stakeholders — connecting the science with domestic economies, supply chains, critical infrastructure and other vulnerabilities, and providing guidance on adaptation and mitigation efforts.

**What does climate change mean for a country and its people?** That is the central question each report sets out to answer, grounding global science in local realities for Bangladesh, South Africa, and Kenya.

## How it was done

Dr. Roz Pidcock, whom InfoDesignLab had met while working on the data visualisations for the Summary for Policymakers of SR1.5, reached out after seeing the work done for the Overshoot Annual Review.

> Information designers, when included from early stages, can facilitate important conversations around content and structure that are not normally part of the scientific process.

The goal was to build the identity of the whole project — including covers and a reports template — setting an approach that both unifies the three reports and differentiates them, acknowledging the particular conditions in each country. InfoDesignLab facilitated the co-design process and created a key signature figure for the executive summary of each report, involving several rounds of iteration with the country teams and editors.

## What conclusions were drawn

Over the past 15 years, InfoDesignLab has been fortunate to witness how the notion that scientists do not care about design or communication has been challenged time and again. This project is a further demonstration: when information designers are brought in from the earliest stages, they can shape not just how findings look, but how they are structured, understood, and ultimately acted upon.

## What was done

**1. The IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C**

The IPCC's assessment reports have been instrumental in shaping international climate policy. Perhaps the most high-profile was the Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C (SR1.5), published in October 2018.

Working closely with the scientist authors, the WGI Technical Support Unit (TSU) and all six Working Group Co-Chairs over a period of 18 months, InfoDesignLab co-designed five key figures for the Summary for Policymakers.

<Figures Figures />

<Figures Figures />

Our specific deliverables for the Special Report on 1.5°C included: co-designing five key SPM figures; real-time editing of figures during the approval session in Incheon, Korea; one summary slide per figure; a user-testing phase; and a COP24 trailer.

With an acute appreciation for the timescales and pressures under which the authors were working, InfoDesignLab's co-design approach aimed to produce figures that would stand up to expert scrutiny while remaining accessible to non-expert audiences.

The design process for the Special Report on 1.5°C involved a user-testing phase carried out by the Tyndall Centre in the UK.

<Figures Figures />

<Figures Figures />

As part of the project outputs, InfoDesignLab produced a trailer presented at COP24 in Katowice, Poland, outlining the main challenges of the report.

***

**2. The IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land**

Working closely with the scientist authors, the WGIII Technical Support Unit (TSU) and all six Working Group Co-Chairs over a period of 18 months, InfoDesignLab co-designed six key figures for the Summary for Policymakers.

<Figures Figures />

Drawing on our experience of the Special Report on 1.5°C, our co-design process incorporated many of the same structural elements. Building on the lessons learnt during the co-design process for the 1.5°C report, we had a better collective understanding of what it takes to co-design figures for global audiences under very tight time constraints.

Our specific outputs for the Special Report on Climate Change and Land included: co-designing six key SPM figures; real-time editing of figures during the approval session in Geneva, Switzerland; and one summary slide per figure.

***

**3. The Working Group I contribution to the AR6 cycle: The Physical Science Basis**

<Figures Figures />

<Figures Figures />

The experience of the special reports indicates a shift towards a design culture that recognises the value of telling a compelling visual story with data.

The full report has been drafted by 234 authors from 65 countries. Authors reviewed a massive body of scientific work; the report has 14,000 cited references to the scientific literature.

The global audience for *The Physical Science Basis* has been extremely broad: 3.3 million is the estimated number of people who shared or engaged with the content in the first weeks after launch.

The digital narrative *Climate Change in Data* builds on and expands the outreach of the report through engaging interactive design.

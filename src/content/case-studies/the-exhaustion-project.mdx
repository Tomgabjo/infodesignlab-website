## What was done

In collaboration with the communication team of the Centre for International Climate Change Research (CICERO), with thirteen partners and thirty-three scientists, InfoDesignLab contributed to the EXHAUSTION project through three interconnected tracks.

**The visual identity** has been the very first track we worked on. Our stance is that every scientific project deserves an identity that is as rigorous and considered as the science itself.

<Figure src={img1} alt="The EXHAUSTION project visual identity" />

**The co-design of a set of data visualisations** that convey the key findings of the project is the second track. We have designed four sets of data visualisations, each as a standalone poster with multiple language versions.

**The co-design process** that we lead for each data visualisation starts with drafting a brief alongside the communications team and the relevant scientific teams.

*We Breathe Climate Change* is the theme that connects the data-visualisations packages. We identified this theme by synthesising the scientific findings of the project.

## How it was done

**First set: The context**

The first package was launched in June 2020 and developed through a co-design process that lasted six months. The intent was to explain the dual relationship between climate change and air pollution.

Because of human activities such as transport, agriculture and energy use, we pump pollutants (such as gases and particulate matter) and greenhouse gases (such as CO₂ and methane) into the atmosphere.

On one hand these emissions build up in the atmosphere causing climate change — acting much like a blanket trapping heat. On the other hand, the same emissions cause air pollution, with immediate health impacts.

The problem is that climate change worsens the health impacts of air pollution. Climate change leads to more frequent heatwaves, which increase the concentration of ground-level ozone.

Climate change also causes changes in air pollution which amplify the health impacts through increased fire seasons, more intense pollen seasons, and greater dust storms.

The visualisation has been translated in several languages and launched on social media with a short animation.

**Second set: The size of the problem**

The second package was launched in summer 2022. Its intent is to focus on the health impacts of the interaction between climate change and air pollution.

There is an increased risk of heart, respiratory diseases and death due to heat. The risk is enhanced when we are exposed to a combination of heat and air pollution.

**Third set: The solution and the future**

The intent of the third package is to shift the attention towards the future and the solutions, with a special focus on mitigation and adaptation.

**Mitigation:** The goal is to convey that by the end of the century climate change may have claimed over 2 million additional premature deaths annually in Europe due to heat alone.

Heat and cold extremes are causing premature deaths in cities across Europe and climate change is expected to impact the urban heat island effect in cities.

**Adaptation:** The goal is to convey that policies that reduce our vulnerability to climate change and increase our capacity to adapt are the most effective tools we have.

**Fourth set: The cost of inaction**

Climate change connects all aspects of the environment, everywhere, and no individual, no community and no country can escape it.

The macro-economic ripple effects of inaction extend far beyond health: reduced labour productivity, increased burden on healthcare systems, and growing inequality between those who can afford to adapt and those who cannot.

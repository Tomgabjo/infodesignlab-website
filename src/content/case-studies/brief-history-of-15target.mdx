## What was done

In summer 2018 Christian Bjørnæs, the communication director at CICERO, the Centre for Norwegian International Climate Research, reached out to InfoDesignLab to explore the possibility of a collaboration. Scientist Monica Bjermeland was at the end of exploring a research question in connection with the launch of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C.

The question at the heart of that research was: what are the origins of the 1.5°C target?

Monica had visited the UN's annual "summer summit" on climate change in Bonn, Germany. This was her first personal meeting with the people behind the target, and she was struck not only by the complexity of the process itself, but by the human dimension running through it:

> Not only by the multiple rooms, corridors and various formal and informal meetings taking place at all hours — she wrote — but by the thought that here were people who had dedicated much of their life's work to understanding and communicating the risks of global warming, and yet were little known outside of academia.

The project set out to bring that human story to a wider audience, tracing the rise of the 1.5°C target from scientific groundwork to global policy prominence.

## How it was done

The challenge for InfoDesignLab was to dig into the qualitative data and, by co-designing with the scientists, to explore different ways of representing the story. The interview subjects ranged across a wide cast of researchers and advocates: from Adelle Thomas, assistant professor at the University of Bahamas and senior research associate at Climate Analytics, to Chris Field, founding director of the Stanford Woods Institute.

The initial idea was to do a thorough analysis of the interviews, highlight key indicators, and use those indicators to build a matrix and a timeline simultaneously. It was a strong starting point — but after listening carefully to the audio recordings and working through the material with Monica and Christian, the team decided to abandon the matrix concept.

> It was a revelatory moment to realise that there was one common element behind the history of the 1.5°C target. Its rise to prominence was a story of people.

That insight became the seed of the timeline. In exploring the history of the 1.5°C target, readers discover that this target is, in fact, the result of sustained, collective effort by researchers and advocates who worked across decades and institutional boundaries to put it on the global agenda.

## By whom

The project was produced for CICERO in collaboration with Monica Bjermeland, who led the underlying research, and Christian Bjørnæs, CICERO's communication director.

## What conclusions were drawn

The project also embarked on portraying the Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS), a coalition of 44 small island and low-lying coastal countries that, despite their tiny greenhouse gas emissions, face devastating impacts from climate change. Sea level rise accelerated by the melting of glaciers due to rising global temperatures has made these islands some of the most vulnerable places on the planet — and it was their sustained advocacy that kept the 1.5°C threshold alive in international negotiations.

## What was done

The Water We Eat is an infographic story that explains the revolutionary concept of Virtual Water — visualising the link between water scarcity and what we eat.

Based on the data published by the Water Footprint Network (Prof. Arjen Hoekstra) and the book *Virtual Water* (Prof Tony Allan), this digital story makes the hidden water cost of food visible.

If we see a packet of pasta we see just that. We forget about all the water used in growing the wheat, producing the packaging, and transporting it.

<Figure src={img1} alt="The Water We Eat — key figure" />

In The Water We Eat every sentence and every data visualisation is the result of a 9-month co-design process aimed at connecting the scientific data with the lived experience of the reader.

When it was first launched, The Water We Eat was the first animated-scrollable data story.

## What conclusions were drawn

After the launch, the project was featured in The Guardian, Huffington Post, Le Monde, Fast Company, Brain Picker.

What blew us away was the feedback we received by email, Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn, every year since the launch, from people who said it changed their behaviour.

A scientist from Norway wrote that she had been thinking *about becoming a vegetarian for several years*, but The Water We Eat is what finally pushed her to make the change.

> The presentation made me see how changing our habits can affect our life, our health, our planet, every day.

A policy maker from India wrote that *during the negotiations at COP 21, the UN Climate Change Conference in Paris,* they used The Water We Eat to illustrate what each individual can do.

> Please continue the good work that you are doing. Hoping to see your similar contributions on other issues affecting humanity.

**Five years in the making**

The Water We Eat was launched in 2012 and it was the result of a 5-year journey in the world of water science.

Angela says that in 2006 this quote — *The world is thirsty because it is hungry* — literally dragged her into the world of water science.

Starting her research with the United Nations World Water Development Report, she came across the Water Footprint Research Group at the University of Twente, led by Prof. Arjen Hoekstra.

> She immediately thought that if the reasons of the water crisis lie in everyone's life, shared responsibility and general awareness is the key to change.

Since 2006, starting with her MA thesis published in 2008 and with the use of information design, data visualisation and animation, Angela has been building a body of work at the intersection of science communication and design.

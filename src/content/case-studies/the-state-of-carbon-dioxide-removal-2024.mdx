## What was done

The State of CDR reports are intended to regularly inform researchers, policymakers and practitioners on the state of Carbon Dioxide Removal.

The project is led by leading academics Oliver Geden (German Institute for International and Security Affairs, SWP), Matthew Gidden (Climate Analytics), Steve Smith (Oxford).

The second edition continues the assessment of CDR development, expanding geographical coverage and including new topics.

## How it was done

The design work we did for the second edition, published in June 2024, builds on the efforts made for the first edition.

For the second edition, we focused on updating the four signature visualisations from the previous report with the latest data and introducing a new design.

The visualisations of the executive summary can be seen as milestones for dissemination and decision-making and they are used and re-used in slides, publications, and media.

**Visualisation 1: Carbon dioxide removal is a feature of all 1.5°C scenarios that meet the Paris temperature goals.**

**Visualisation 2: Only a tiny fraction of all CDR results from novel methods.**

<Figure src={img6} alt="Visualisation 2: Only a tiny fraction of all CDR results from novel methods" caption="Visualisation 2 from the executive summary of the State of Carbon Dioxide Removal (2nd edition)" />

**Visualisation 3 (new): Indicators of CDR development show an emerging diversity of conventional and novel CDR methods.**

<Figure src={img7} alt="Visualisation 3: Indicators of CDR development" caption="Visualisation 3 from the executive summary of the State of Carbon Dioxide Removal (2nd edition)" />

**Visualisation 4: There continues to be a gap between the amount of CDR in scenarios and the actual deployment.**

<Figure src={img8} alt="Visualisation 4: Gap between CDR in scenarios and actual deployment" caption="Visualisation 4 from the executive summary of the State of Carbon Dioxide Removal (2nd edition)" />

Among the new additions to the second edition is a Voronoi treemap designed to capture the growing diversity of CDR methods — both conventional and novel — and the indicators used to assess their development.

The Voronoi treemap offers a flexible visual form that can hold many categories simultaneously without imposing a rigid hierarchy, making it well suited to a field as heterogeneous as CDR.

## What was done

> "Dr. Hoenikker used to say that any scientist who couldn't explain to an eight-year-old what he was doing was a charlatan." — Kurt Vonnegut, *Cat's Cradle*

InfoDesignLab brought its design experience to an international collaboration of scientists led by the Norwegian Institute of Public Health. Good health and well-being depends on making good choices. Making good choices, in turn, depends on the ability to access reliable, easy-to-understand information about which treatments and services actually work.

Individuals and organisations across a wide variety of fields are working to enable people to make evidence-informed decisions. This project sits at the intersection of all of them.

<Figure src={img1} alt="Informed Health Choices — learning resources" />

**Learning resources.** One of the specific outputs included developing and testing user-friendly summaries of evidence and priority setting. The project was partnered by universities and non-profit organisations around the world, including in Australia, Chile, China, Ethiopia, Kenya, Norway, Rwanda, Uganda, and the UK. It resulted in multiple publications, including two Lancet papers co-authored by InfoDesignLab's co-founder and scientific director Angela Morelli.

## How it was done

**That's a claim.** A large research team worked for decades to promote informed choices across 14 fields: agriculture, economics, education, environment, justice, nutrition, occupational health, and more. The Key Concepts for Informed Choices are principles for evaluating the trustworthiness of claims about the effects of interventions, tests and more — a shared vocabulary for critical appraisal across disciplines.

**The iSoF and the iEtD.** The interactive Summary of Findings Table (iSoF) is an interactive tool that enables producers to visualise for end-users the key findings from systematic reviews. The interactive Evidence to Decision (iEtD) tool facilitates use of the Evidence to Decision (EtD) frameworks by guideline developers. Together, the tools help guideline panels:

- Deliberate transparently on the evidence and other factors informing a recommendation.
- Communicate the basis of recommendations clearly to end-users.
- Support adaptation of guidelines to different contexts.
- Enable structured dissent where panels do not reach consensus.

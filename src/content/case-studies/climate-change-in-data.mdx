## What was done

Climate Change in Data is a collaboration between InfoDesignLab, the IPCC Working Group I Technical Support Unit (WGI TSU), and Ado, a design studio.

**The background.** Being the information designers responsible for the visualisations of three summaries for policymakers for the IPCC prompted a broader question: how to make complex climate science accessible to wider audiences. The Physical Science Basis is the IPCC Working Group I contribution to the sixth assessment cycle, approved in August 2021. Its scale is formidable: 234 authors, more than 14,000 references, 78,007 review comments — over 1,300 of them directly addressing visualisations — and a final reach of 3.3 million people engaged.

The expectations for these reports are consistently high. With such intense global interest in climate change, the challenge was to communicate the findings of The Physical Science Basis in an engaging, accessible way.

> Tailoring the visualisations we co-designed for the Summary for Policymakers (SPM) into visual formats that resonate with wider audiences was the key challenge.

## How it was done

**The co-design process.** InfoDesignLab facilitated the co-design process over a period of eight months. The ambition was to co-design an outreach product to support the dissemination of the AR6 WGI report findings. Three goals guided the process:

- Make the key findings of The Physical Science Basis comprehensible to non-specialist audiences.
- Develop visual formats that translate SPM figures into a plain-language digital narrative.
- Build a coherent visual identity consistent with the IPCC's existing identity.

The co-design process drew on the expertise of many professionals: scientists, information designers, programmers, UX/UI designers, science communicators, and editors. The creative process involved many rounds of iteration to develop both the visual identity and a plain-language, accessible digital product.

## What was done

![](/images/scientific-american-overshoot/00-img.jpg)

In the summer 2025, Scientific American devoted a double spread to one of the most pressing topics in climate science: climate overshoot.

<Figures cols={2} items={[{src:"/images/scientific-american-overshoot/01-img.png",alt:"Scientific American overshoot spread"},{src:"/images/scientific-american-overshoot/02-img.png",alt:"Scientific American overshoot spread"}]} />

The world's average temperature has increased by more than 1°C since the beginning of the Industrial Revolution. By the time you read this, it may well have increased by 1.5°C.

The IPCC's sixth assessment report makes clear that with every increment of warming, climate extremes and risks increase.

These trends are in stark contrast with the Paris Agreement's ambition to "pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels".

> The "1.5 °C" is a compromise — as Dr Friederike Otto writes — a compromise between death, damage, and loss on the one hand and political feasibility on the other.

Even though exceedance of 1.5°C of global warming is now largely inevitable, such exceedance can be limited in magnitude and duration.

**Overshoot describes a trajectory where global temperatures rise beyond the 1.5°C threshold of warming but eventually return below that threshold through removal of CO₂ from the atmosphere.**

Admitting that overshoot is increasingly likely is not a justification for delay. On the contrary, it calls for faster action.

## How it was done

The spread we designed for Scientific American builds on the foundations of a year-long collaboration InfoDesignLab carried out with leading overshoot scientists.

The Scientific American spread is a continuation of that work, translating its insights for a broader public audience.

We structured the narrative around five key "atoms":

* The current trajectory of global temperatures and the 1.5°C threshold
* What overshoot means — exceeding and returning below the threshold
* The role of carbon dioxide removal in enabling a return below 1.5°C
* The risks and consequences of spending time above the threshold
* What faster action now means for the magnitude and duration of overshoot

This progression created a balance of warning and possibility. The **left-hand side** introduced the concept step by step; the **right-hand side** explored its implications.

The double spread is the result of a co-design process. Andy Reisinger and the core scientific team played a central role in shaping the narrative.

Among the atoms, one of the most distinctive is the one of multiple overlapping lines, first developed in the Annual Review paper.

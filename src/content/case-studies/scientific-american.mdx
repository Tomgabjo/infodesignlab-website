## What was done

Our first encounter with Jen Christiansen, science communicator, author, and editor at Scientific American, happened when she featured our work on the Overshoot Annual Review.

Collaboration, systems-oriented design and participation within visualising science have been very central to the work we do, and these are also values central to the work of Scientific American.

On December 6, 2023, we received an email from Jen asking, "Available for a Scientific American project?". Planetary boundaries.

Jen was offering the opportunity to work on a graphic spread accompanying an article written by Prof. Joyeeta Gupta, co-director of the Amsterdam Institute for Advanced Metropolitan Solutions, and Prof. Johan Rockström, Director of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research.

The context for the article was that in 2009 an international group of scientists famously determined that society had transgressed four planetary boundaries. In 2023, a landmark study updated that assessment: six of the eight boundaries have now been exceeded.

## How it was done

Regardless of a project's scale, length, and the number of people involved, **research**, **intent**, and **user inclusion** are the three gates every design process must pass through.

**The research**

The deadline was tight, but the dedication of the Scientific American team and the responsiveness of the scientists in answering our research questions made the process work.

We decided to dive not only into the evidence to understand the assessment and its dimensions, but also into *the groundbreaking story behind it* — to understand what it took to bring such a study to life.

We see every project not only as an opportunity to visualise the findings, but also as a chance to highlight the scientific process and the people behind it.

**The intent**

The interactions with Jen and the scientists helped us answer many questions and define a very clear intent for the narrative.

We worked to get the intent across by visualising:

- The safe boundaries — the limits within which humanity can safely operate
- The safe *and just* boundaries — extending the framework to include justice criteria
- Where we are now — the current state of transgression across all eight boundaries
- The behind-the-scenes efforts — the scientific process that made this assessment possible
- The justice criteria — what it means for people across the world to live within planetary boundaries

<Figure src={img7} alt="Final spread for Scientific American, Safe and Just Boundaries" />

**The user inclusion**

Jen's extraordinary design skills and her elegance in handling communications, feedback, support, and suggestions have been a constant source of inspiration throughout the project.

The involvement of Scientific American reviewers and the light user testing we conducted internally guided our decisions and improved the final outcome.
